COURSE OBJECTIVES
  1. Review mathematical foundations and computational machines.
  2. Design DFAs and NFAs and convert between models.
  3. Use regular expressions and prove when languages are not regular.
  4. Design CFGs, PDAs, and Turing machines for languages.
  5. Understand computability and introductory complexity.
BY THE END, YOU CAN
  1. Apply logic and mathematics to finite automata and their variants.
  2. Work with regular expressions, pumping lemma, and Myhill–Nerode.
  3. Construct and simplify context-free grammars.
  4. Model language problems with pushdown automata.
  5. Build Turing machines and classify basic complexity problems.
FIVE UNITS · 40 HOURS TOTAL
UNIT I · 08 HOURSFormal languages & finite automata＋

Sets, alphabets, strings, empty string, substrings, concatenation, languages and their operations; DFA, NFA and ε-NFA conversions; DFA minimization; Moore and Mealy machines and conversion between them.

Case studies Vending machine · Spell checker · ATM PIN validation
UNIT II · 06 HOURSRegular expressions & languages＋

RE operators, precedence and algebraic laws; Kleene’s theorem; RE↔automaton conversions and Arden’s theorem; pumping lemma; closure and decision properties; Myhill–Nerode theorem.

Case studies Variable-name validation · Matching a word · Email validation
UNIT III · 09 HOURSContext-free grammars & languages＋

CFGs, derivations and parse trees, ambiguity, grammar construction and simplification; Chomsky and Greibach normal forms; pumping lemma and closure properties for CFLs; Chomsky hierarchy and applications.

Case studies Arithmetic expressions · A simple programming language
UNIT IV · 09 HOURSPushdown automata＋

Formal PDA definition; acceptance by final state or empty stack; nondeterministic PDA; equivalence of PDA and CFG; PDA and context-free languages; introduction to Post machines.

Case studies Top-down and bottom-up parsing · Syntax checking · XML/HTML tag validation
UNIT V · 08 HOURSTuring machines & computability＋

TM model, representations, instantaneous descriptions, transitions, deterministic TMs and construction; universal TM and Church–Turing thesis; halting, decidable and undecidable problems; reducibility, recursive enumerability, time and space, P, NP and NP-completeness.

Case studies TM language recognition · Comparing machine variants · Analyzing the halting problem
